# Research: idle / incremental games (Oct 2026)

One page, to be deleted once the spec exists. Full report and sources: `reports/Idle incremental game landscape.md`.

## The market in one paragraph

Steam idlers are booming and flooded: the "idler" tag went from 100 games in 2020 to 1,811 released in 2026 so far, many with the same blue, AI-generated look. The hits come in two formats: **short premium incrementals** ($3–$8, 3–10 h, a real ending) and **cozy desktop companions** (a strip of the screen you leave running). Paying once at $5–$8 beats F2P on Steam. The math is solved (Pecorella's public cost and prestige curves); **theme, art identity and the pacing of reveals** are what set games apart, and they're the only parts that can't be cloned in a few months.

## Reference games

| Game | What to take from it |
|---|---|
| **A Dark Room** / **Universal Paperclips** | The growing system *is* the story: start nearly empty, each unlock is a story beat, the main resource changes meaning, and a late reveal turns on the player. Huge on the web, weak on Steam because text doesn't sell in a 120px thumbnail. |
| **Progress Knight** / **Increlution** | The reset makes sense in the story: you age, die and pass something on. Rebirth unlocks at 65 and is forced at 70; later lives open moral forks (the Evil path). The weak spot is Increlution's story, still unfinished years into Early Access. |
| **Melvor Idle** | Depth and respect for your time win long-term (~16K reviews, $9.99 + $4.99 expansions, no microtransactions). It has no prestige, just skill caps and mastery. Its 2024 expansion fell to 59% positive because the new systems didn't connect to existing ones. |
| **The Gnorp Apologue** | A solo developer, $7, 100K+ copies in its first month. Growth is shown as a swarm of creatures, so each multiplier is something you watch happen, and you never control an avatar. |
| **Rusty's Retirement** | A solo developer, ~550K copies, **no plot**. A warm pixel-art mascot in the bottom third of your screen. It proves the cozy desktop format, and that format now has many farm and pet copies. |

## Possible hooks

The gap: games praised for story mostly sold on the web, and the cozy Steam hits have almost no story. **No hit yet combines an incremental whose story *is* its progression with art that reads instantly on a store page.** Directions worth brainstorming:

- **A cozy surface with a darker turn:** the look that sells on Steam, plus the reveal structure critics praise. The twist has to be kept out of the marketing.
- **A reset that makes sense in the story:** the reset as the climax or an enemy's act, death plus inheritance, or a time loop that keeps knowledge. Never a plain "prestige points" button.
- **A theme that mirrors optimizing:** greed and runaway AI are taken (Gnorp, Paperclips, Cookie Clicker), so it needs a fresh subject.
- **Desktop strip outside farm/pet/mining:** a lighthouse, observatory, shop and so on. A gap the research suggests, not proven demand.
- **Handmade art with a mascot,** which reads as "made by a person" in a sea of AI-generated idlers.

## What players hate

1. **Slow, confusing openings.** Players under 2 hours are the most negative group (Unnamed Space Idle).
2. **Walls, grind, and waiting with nothing to decide.**
3. **"Idle" games that must stay open:** weak offline progress, offline caps, games that end up needing to run 24/7 (IdleOn, Leaf Blower Revolution).
4. **New systems that don't feed the existing economy:** currencies that sit unused, the Melvor expansion.
5. **Pay-to-win and microtransactions;** F2P item-drop games get called scams (Banana).
6. **Resets that feel like a downgrade,** and themes that aren't part of the mechanics.
7. **Unreadable UI:** tiny text in small windows, confusing letter suffixes (aa, ab…) on big numbers, cluttered tabs.
8. **Unfinished or abandoned stories.**
9. **Generic look:** blue sci-fi dashboards, AI art.

## Decide first, before mechanics

- Authored story with an ending (3–10 h, ~$5–$8) **or** endless ambient lore (cozy, desktop)?
- What is the reset in the story?
- Does the art pass three tests: readable at 120px, holds a glance on a second screen, and every order of magnitude shows up as more stuff on screen?
