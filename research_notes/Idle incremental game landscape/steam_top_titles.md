# Top and Trending Idle/Incremental Games on Steam (as of October 2026)

> **Method note for the report writer (applies to every section below):** This session's network blocked direct page fetches. SteamSpy API, store.steampowered.com, steam250.com, topincrementalgames.com and incrementalgames.org all failed with DNS errors or proxy 403s. So every figure below comes from **web-search result excerpts** (store snapshots, tracker pages, news articles) collected on **2026-10-16**, not from pages I loaded and checked myself. Review counts on Steam store snapshots are usually **English-only** counts and may be weeks old. Copies sold and revenue from Gamalytic, Raijin, games-stats, Steam Revenue Calculator, Sensor Tower/VG Insights and AppMagic are **third-party estimates**, and they often disagree by 2–3x. Developer-reported sales figures are flagged as such.

## Q1. Which idle/incremental/clicker games are the top sellers, most-reviewed and highest-rated on Steam?

### Takeaway
By review volume and revenue, the genre is led by a few long-running premium titles: Clicker Heroes (~62k reviews), Cookie Clicker (~55k–93k reviews; est. 720k+ copies) and Melvor Idle (~16k reviews). Next come premium "desktop companion" and "bite-sized" hits: Rusty's Retirement (550k copies, developer-reported), Gnorp Apologue and Nodebuster. Free-to-play games lead on concurrent players: Bongo Cat has an all-time peak of 194,508 and still draws ~150–190k in 2026, and Banana peaked at 700k–917k but mostly from bots. Review-score rankings such as Steam250 are topped by small cheap or free 2025–26 games (Bills Must Be Paid, Magic Archery, Berry Bury Berry, Tower Wizard, Nodebuster).

### Cited Findings
**Ranking lists (as of Oct 2026)**
- The Steam250 "Incremental" tag ranking, which is based on reviews, is topped by Bills Must Be Paid, Magic Archery and Berry Bury Berry. Clicker Heroes (2015) also ranks high with ~62,000 reviews. — [Steam250 Incremental](https://steam250.com/tag/incremental) (from a search excerpt; the page itself failed to load)
- Incrementalgames.org's "Active incremental games on Steam" list was updated Oct 15, 2026 and ranks 32 games by live ratings. Nodebuster is #1, Tower Wizard #2 and Magic Archery #3. — [incrementalgames.org](https://incrementalgames.org/best/active-incremental-games-steam)

### Inferences
- "Top" means different things by metric. Review totals favor games from 2015–2021 (Clicker Heroes, Cookie Clicker, Melvor). Concurrent players favor F2P idlers with item drops or tradable cosmetics (Bongo Cat, Banana), where bot and farming activity inflates CCU. Review-score rankings favor cheap or free short games from 2025–26 with fewer reviews.
- Bongo Cat is likely the largest *ongoing* idle title on Steam by concurrent players in 2026. Its ~150–190k range sits well above any premium incremental.

## Q3. Which 2024–2026 launches broke out, and why?

### Takeaway
The best-documented breakouts fall into three groups:
- **Desktop-overlay idlers:** Rusty's Retirement at 550K copies; Bongo Cat at a 194K CCU peak.
- **Cheap, short "bite-sized" incrementals:** Gnorp Apologue at 100K+ in month one; Nodebuster; Tower Wizard (96% positive, $2.99, ~5-hour run); Magic Archery (free, ~1 hour).
- **F2P Steam-Inventory clickers:** Banana, inflated by bots.

The named drivers are a novel format (always on screen), low price and short length, and free demos. Banana is credited with bringing many new players to the genre.

### Cited Findings
- Chris Zukowski (How To Market A Game) lists idle/incremental among genres currently surging. He credits the viral **BANANA** with introducing many players to the genre and notes "dozens of hit idle games since then" — [howtomarketagame.com, Nov 4 2025](https://howtomarketagame.com/2025/11/04/the-optimistic-case-that-indie-games-are-in-a-golden-age-right-now/).
- Rusty's Retirement went from 100K copies in 5 days to 550K. Its developer credits the "bottom of your screen" format — [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/simulation/after-selling-100000-copies-of-its-idle-stardew-valley-like-rustys-retirement-dev-celebrates-spawning-a-genre-of-games-that-run-at-the-bottom-of-your-screen/). GameDiscoverCo published a dedicated breakdown, "How Rusty's Retirement idle-farmed its way to 300k+ Steam sales" — [GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/how-rustys-retirement-idle-farmed).
- Gnorp Apologue sold >120K copies in its first month; GameDiscoverCo's Simon Carless analyzed it as a case study — [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/simoncarless_how-did-the-gnorp-apologue-sell-120k-copies-activity-7155318508615024641-XHcR).
- Bongo Cat (F2P) reached its 194,508 peak 60 days after launch, after passing 100K and then 150K — [SteamDB/Steam News](https://store.steampowered.com/news/group/4777282/view/539975439117977011).
- Banana: up to ~700K–917K CCU in June 2024, driven by Steam Market item drops and bot farming — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banana_(2024_video_game)); [Spilled](https://spilled.gg/banana-game-steam/); [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/steams-second-most-played-game-right-now-is-reportedly-a-bot-filled-marketplace-that-can-get-you-banned/).
- A "bite-sized incremental games" discussion thread on ResetEra shows the short-incremental format is now a named category among core gamers — [ResetEra](https://www.resetera.com/threads/bite-sized-incremental-games.1087230/).
- Tower Wizard: $2.99, ~5-hour run, 96% positive across 7,241 reviews — [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3372980/Tower_Wizard/); [Tessier](http://www.jeremietessier.com/reviews/2025/10/25/i-look-at-tower-wizard).
- Dwarf Eats Mountain (2026) launched after a February free demo, but its scale is disputed: 19K vs 161K copies — [Raijin](https://raijin.gg/app/4078200/Dwarf_Eats_Mountain); [Sensor Tower](https://app.sensortower.com/vgi/game/dwarf-eats-mountain).
- AppMagic says the Steam idle market is shifting toward desktop-based, system-driven idle titles and away from purely mobile-style clickers — [AppMagic](https://appmagic.rocks/research/idle-steam-games-2026).

### Inferences
- The common pattern is low commitment and low price: short completion times (1–5 hours), prices of $0–$5, or an always-on-screen format. These make impulse purchases and word of mouth easy. That fits the "bite-sized" label used by players and press.
- The Banana and Bongo Cat style of F2P plus tradable cosmetics produces huge CCU but weak revenue signals. The CCU numbers should not be read as commercial success comparable to premium titles.

### Gaps
- No primary source in this session documented streamer or YouTuber coverage, or Steam Next Fest results, for specific breakouts (Nodebuster, Gnorp, Tower Wizard). The GameDiscoverCo and LinkedIn posts probably cover this but could not be read in full.
- No Ball x Pit data. Whether it counts as incremental-adjacent is unverified.

## Q4. Typical price points: is F2P+IAP or premium more common or successful on Steam?

### Takeaway
Premium dominates by revenue and sales evidence. The documented hits mostly cost **$2.99–$9.99**: Tower Wizard $2.99, Melvor and Dwarf Eats Mountain $9.99, Rusty's Retirement at ~$5 net per unit. F2P leads on raw player counts (Bongo Cat, Banana, IdleOn, Cell to Singularity, Revolution Idle), but its review scores and monetization reception are weaker. IdleOn sits at 75–79% positive, and Revolution Idle is criticized for intrusive monetization.

### Cited Findings
- Melvor Idle: $9.99 base plus three $4.99 expansions (premium plus DLC) — [Overbaked Studio](https://overbaked.studio/blog/idle-games-on-steam-without-microtransactions/).
- Dwarf Eats Mountain: $9.99, 20% launch discount. Some reviewers call $10 too high for the content — [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/4078200/Dwarf_Eats_Mountain?snr=1_25_4__318); [Steam reviews](https://steamcommunity.com/app/4078200/reviews/?browsefilter=toprated).
- Tower Wizard: $2.99 — [TopIncrementalGames](https://www.topincrementalgames.com/category/incremental/steam).
- Rusty's Retirement: ~$5 net revenue per unit after refunds, discounts and regional pricing (GameDiscoverCo estimate), plus a Supporter Pack DLC (~36K units) — [GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/how-rustys-retirement-idle-farmed).
- Gnorp: ~$1.41M gross / ~$416K net estimate, against 235K copies (Raijin), which implies roughly $6 gross per unit — [SRC](https://steam-revenue-calculator.com/app/1473350/(the)-gnorp-apologue); [Raijin](https://raijin.gg/app/1473350/the_Gnorp_Apologue).
- Magic Archery: free — [Steam reviews](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2905170/reviews/).
- F2P titles with large review bases score lower: IdleOn 75% of 21,848 — [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1476970/Legends_of_IdleOn__Idle_MMO/); Cell to Singularity 85% of 11,378 — [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/977400/Cell_to_Singularity__Evolution_Never_Ends/). Premium titles score higher: Cookie Clicker 96%, Tower Wizard 96%, Unnamed Space Idle 92%, Antimatter Dimensions 91%.
- A 2026 article covers "idle games on Steam without microtransactions" as a selling point, which suggests players value a premium, no-IAP model — [Overbaked Studio](https://overbaked.studio/blog/idle-games-on-steam-without-microtransactions/).

### Inferences
- On Steam, a "pay once, no IAP" premium model at $3–$10 looks like the dominant *successful* model for incrementals. F2P works mainly when the game is a CCU and community phenomenon (Bongo Cat, Banana) or a long-lived live-service idle MMO (IdleOn). This is drawn from the review-score gap and the documented premium sales, not from a systematic dataset.
- Antimatter Dimensions and Unnamed Space Idle are also F2P-style or ad-free web ports (from my background knowledge; not confirmed in this session's sources). The report should check pricing on the store pages.

### Gaps
- No systematic breakdown found of the share of F2P vs premium titles among the top Idler/Incremental games by revenue. AppMagic's top-50 list would answer this but was not readable.
- Median or average price for the tag was not found.

## Q5. Size of the idle/incremental tag market (SteamDB / Gamalytic / VG Insights / SteamSpy)

### Takeaway
Only weak, indirect sizing was found. A secondary summary of AppMagic data calls Steam idle games a **~$59M category in 2026**, which is unconfirmed in AppMagic's own text. IncrementalGames.org tracks ~29,000 incremental titles across all platforms. No SteamDB, Gamalytic, VG Insights or SteamSpy tag-level totals could be retrieved.

### Cited Findings
- NextBigGames, summarizing AppMagic, describes idle games on Steam as a "$59M PC gaming category in 2026" — [NextBigGames, May 9 2026](https://nextbiggames.com/2026/05/09/idle-games-on-steam-revenue-trends-2026/). The figure is not confirmed in AppMagic's own report — [AppMagic](https://appmagic.rocks/research/idle-steam-games-2026).
- IncrementalGames.org tracks ~29,000 incremental titles across platforms, not only Steam — [incrementalgames.org](https://incrementalgames.org/).
- Generic "idle games market" reports (Growth Market Reports, MarketIntelo, DataIntelo) give conflicting segment shares: 40.1% vs 17.3% for "incremental." They appear low quality and are mobile-dominated — [Growth Market Reports](https://growthmarketreports.com/report/idle-games-market); [MarketIntelo](https://marketintelo.com/report/idle-games-market); [DataIntelo](https://dataintelo.com/report/idle-games-market).
- Wikipedia's definitions: "clicker" games stress active input, "idle" games stress automation, and the genre is often traced to Progress Quest (2002) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incremental_game).

### Inferences
- If the ~$59M per year figure is accurate, it is small next to single hit games. Rusty's Retirement alone (~550K copies at ~$5 net, about $2.75M net, or more gross) and Cookie Clicker (est. $8–22M lifetime gross) would be meaningful shares of it. This suggests the $59M may cover only a subset, such as recent releases or a top-50 list, or be an annual figure. Treat it with caution.
