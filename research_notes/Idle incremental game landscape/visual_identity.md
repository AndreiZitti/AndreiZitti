## Taxonomy of visual styles (with examples) and which styles are associated with Steam commercial success

### Takeaway
On Steam in 2024–2026, the commercially strongest idle games are visual, cozy and small-footprint: pixel-art or illustrated desktop companions, plus lofi "room" games. Spirit City: Lofi Sessions, Cast n Chill, Rusty's Retirement and Bongo Cat lead on revenue or players. Minimalist geometric/neon games (Nodebuster, Revolution Idle) are the cheap, high-volume breakout style. Pure-text and spreadsheet games succeed mainly on the web, or on Steam when the game has an existing brand (Melvor Idle via RuneScape/Jagex, Cookie Clicker via its web fame).

### Inferences
- The top revenue tier on Steam (Spirit City, Cast n Chill, Rusty's, Bongo Cat) is dominated by warm, character-driven, cozy visuals that look good when watched passively. Abstract or text UIs are not in that tier. Dense menu UIs (Melvor, Antimatter Dimensions) still sell solidly but seem to rely on depth and community reputation rather than visual appeal.
- Minimal geometric/neon is the most cost-efficient style: Nodebuster sold an estimated 200K–500K copies at $2.99 with "simple" graphics. But it is easy to clone (see the copycat accusation), so on its own it is a weak moat.
- The biggest risk in 2026 is looking generic. With roughly 1,800 idlers a year, a shared blue-UI look and a lot of AI art, a handmade, recognizable style is now a way to stand out, as PC Gamer's praise for handmade pixel art suggests.
- Gnorp shows that "programmer art taken up a notch" plus heavy on-screen activity can compete with full illustration, if the art serves watching the numbers grow and avoids avatar control.

## How successful games make "numbers going up" feel satisfying visually (juice)

### Takeaway
Juice means "maximum output for minimum input": tweening, squash and stretch, scale pops, screenshake and sound layered on simple actions. In idle games it substitutes for player agency. Gnorp's lesson is to show the economy as on-screen activity (many creatures hitting the rock) rather than as a passive avatar. Nodebuster's is that simple shapes plus strong effects read as polished.

### Cited Findings
- "Juice It or Lose It" (Martin Jonasson and Petri Purho, GDC Europe 2012) defines juice as "maximum output for minimum input", usually visual or audio feedback. It demos tweening, stretching/squeezing, screenshake for impact, and scaling things up or down on events, all on a Breakout clone. Purho said a juicy game "feels more professional". — [roblog summary](https://roblog.co.uk/2024/03/juicy-games/); [Game Developer video](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/video-is-your-game-juicy-enough-)
- Counterpoint: Folmer Kelly's talk "Don't Juice It or Lose It" argues that heavy juice can cost immersion. — [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/video-indies-resist-the-urge-to-juice-it-or-lose-it-)
- Gnorp: Myco's aim was a *visual* incremental, and they rejected character control because it gets in the way of the enjoyment of watching numbers grow. Reviewers note the colors avoid overcrowding the screen despite the activity. — [Game Developer interview](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/interview-the-gnorp-apologue); [ComicBuzz](https://comicbuzz.com/the-gnorp-apologue-review/)
- Nodebuster: the player hovers the cursor over enemies while attacks fire automatically, enemies drop resources, bosses unlock levels, and there is prestige. The reviewer found it relaxing and "well-polished", with simple graphics lifted by the effects and mood. — [Elliot C Smith](https://www.elliotcsmith.com/nodebuster/)
- Ballionaire's core loop is balls bouncing through triggers that score dollars toward a money goal, and reviewers praise the "chaos". — [God is a Geek](https://godisageek.com/reviews/ballionaire-review/); [GamingOnLinux](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2024/10/be-careful-with-pachinko-roguelike-ballionaire-as-it-might-suck-away-your-entire-week/page=1/)
- Cookie Clicker's Steam version added music, which the browser original lacked. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cookie_Clicker)

### Inferences
- In incrementals, the screen has to make each order-of-magnitude jump visible: more units, more particles, bigger pops. A label change alone is not enough. Gnorp's swarm and Ballionaire's chaos both turn multipliers into spectacle.
- Desktop companions need restrained juice (gentle idle animation, soft sounds). Screenshake or loud pops on a window someone keeps open while working would likely annoy; Kelly's "don't juice" critique applies most here.

### Gaps
- No primary source found for Nodebuster's or Ballionaire's own juice techniques (dev interviews or GDC talks). No data quantifying how particles or number pops affect retention in idle games.
- Peglin-likes beyond Ballionaire were not researched.

## Steam capsule art, trailers and screenshots, and whether text-only games are disadvantaged on Steam

### Takeaway
Steam's storefront is image-first. A capsule gets about one second in the Discovery Queue and must still read at about 120px, and screenshots must show real gameplay. That structurally disadvantages text and spreadsheet incrementals relative to the web, where A Dark Room and Universal Paperclips became famous. The evidence fits: A Dark Room has strong reviews but under 20K estimated Steam owners, and Paperclips never shipped on Steam.

### Cited Findings
- The following points come from Steam-page guides. The search summary did not map each point to a specific guide:
  - A player scrolling the Discovery Queue gives a capsule "maybe one second".
  - Review the small capsule at real size ("a logo that reads at 1232 pixels may become lint at 120 pixels").
  - Steam wants screenshots that show the game, not concept art or pre-rendered stills. Upload at least 5, and 8–12 is recommended.
  - Build one wide key art with safe zones and crop every capsule from it.
  - "A well-composed screenshot can be a better capsule base than mediocre custom art."
  - Sources: [steamcapsule.com guide](https://www.steamcapsule.com/guide); [steampageanalyzer](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/steam-capsule-design-guide); [presskit.gg](https://presskit.gg/field-guides/steam-page-optimization-guide); [savedpixel Next Fest Oct 2026 checklist](https://savedpixel.com/blog/steam-next-fest-visual-checklist-stylized-indie-games/)
- Rusty's Retirement's hook was largely visual: the "takes up the bottom third of your screen" pitch "really beguiled people". Streamer Pirate Software praised it, and Twitter/Instagram Reels and TikTok posts helped. A side effect: most streams were filed under "Just Chatting" because the game isn't full-screen. — [GameDiscoverCo, "How Rusty's Retirement idle-farmed its way to 300k+ Steam sales"](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/how-rustys-retirement-idle-farmed)
- Desktop-idler demos get judged in seconds. PC Gamer's Andrea Shearon (17 June 2026) ran 45 Steam Next Fest idle demos at once on a second screen, closed any that didn't hold her interest "within seconds", and kept 11. — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/i-ran-45-idle-game-demos-from-steam-next-fest-all-at-once-for-a-desktop-companion-battle-royale/); [games.gg](https://games.gg/news/steam-next-fest-idle-game-demos/)
- Text-only on Steam: A Dark Room has 91% positive reviews but an estimated 0–20K owners. Universal Paperclips is not on Steam. — [Is There Any Deal](https://isthereanydeal.com/product/018d9386-112a-73cf-ba55-c8b3b47afdf1/); [SteamSpy](https://www.steamspy.com/app/2460660); [Dinogame](https://dinogame.gg/blog/browser-games-that-became-steam-games/)

### Inferences
- For a desktop companion, the screenshot to lead with is the game sitting on a real desktop, next to a spreadsheet or browser. The context is the pitch, as Rusty's showed.
- A text or menu incremental going to Steam needs a stand-in visual hook: a strong logo, a distinctive palette or typography (CRT, terminal glow), or a mid-game screenshot full of activity. Otherwise the capsule fails at 120px. Launching free on the web first and porting to Steam later (Cookie Clicker, Antimatter Dimensions, Melvor) works around this.
- Desktop idlers should be designed for a "second-screen glance test": readable and appealing within seconds in a small window.

### Gaps
- No quantitative study found comparing wishlist or conversion rates for text versus graphical idle games on Steam.
- Zukowski's posts on capsule art and desktop idlers could not be read in full (blocked). I found no dedicated HTMAG post on desktop idler art style.
- No trailer-specific findings for idle games.

## The rise of desktop companion / "productivity idler" games (2024–2026)

### Takeaway
Rusty's Retirement (April 2024; over 500K copies sold) started a wave: a small pixel-art window along the bottom or side of the screen, little input, built to run during work, and tied to streaming. Spirit City: Lofi Sessions (over $5.3M) and Bongo Cat (free, about 190K peak concurrent players) are the biggest outcomes. Ropuka's Idle Island, Tiny Pasture and Desktop Defender carried the format into 2025–2026. PC Gamer called the desktop-pet renaissance 2025's most surprising cozy trend.

### Cited Findings
- **Rusty's Retirement:** a thin bar along the bottom of the screen. The player orders Rusty to plant or clear crops while selling and farm management are automated. It sold over 500K Steam copies, and PC Gamer praised its passive design and slow, relaxing pace. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rusty%27s_Retirement)
  - The Steam store says it is built "to sit comfortably at the bottom of your screen allowing you to do other tasks", and it has Twitch integration where chat commands set robots' tasks. — [Steam store](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2666510/Rustys_Retirement/); [GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/how-rustys-retirement-idle-farmed)
  - It is pixel art: BGR described it as a "pixelated farm on your desktop". — [BGR](https://www.bgr.com/entertainment/ingenious-idle-game-puts-a-pixelated-farm-on-your-desktop/)
  - A reviewer called the "quarter-screen game" idea unique and the art charming, but faulted the lack of a tutorial. — [Comfy Cozy Gaming, May 2024](https://www.comfycozygaming.com/2024/05/03/rustys-retirement-review/)
- **Bongo Cat** (Marcel Zurawka and Julius Krüger, published by Irox Games): free on Steam from March 2025. It sits above the taskbar and taps for every keystroke or click, and the counter unlocks cats, hats and emojis. Its peak passed 100K concurrent players on 8 Apr 2025. All-time peaks are reported at about 187,909 (SteamDB), 193,859 (SteamPulse) and 194,508 (Tracker.gg). VGI estimates about 5.4M "units", which for a free app means owners. — [Vandal](https://vandal.elespanol.com/noticia/1350779801/el-gato-que-golpea-tu-barra-de-tareas-asi-es-bongo-cat-el-nuevo-fenomeno-de-steam/); [SteamPulse](https://www.steampulse.org/game/3419430); [Tracker.gg](https://tracker.gg/population/steam/3419430); [Phong Vu (SteamDB)](https://phongvu.vn/cong-nghe/game-vo-danh-mien-phi-gay-sot-tren-steam/)
- **Ropuka's Idle Island** (Moczan, Little Chmura, Begoña Pereda; 29 Jan 2025): a frog cuts grass to fund upgrades and decorations on a movable, resizable island that can sit anywhere on the screen. - **Why they work:** PC players often work while gaming or leave games running passively. — [NextBigGames, May 2026](https://nextbiggames.com/2026/05/09/idle-games-on-steam-revenue-trends-2026/). For Rusty's, being able to play a game in a window while doing other things was the core hook. — [GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/how-rustys-retirement-idle-farmed)

### Inferences
- The typical art style is pixel art or cute, chunky illustration; animals and robots are common. A transparent or borderless window, a strip or corner footprint, slow ambient animation and lofi sound complete the look. Pixel art suits a small footprint because it stays legible when scaled down by whole-number factors.
- Being cheap and fast to make, which Desktop Defender shows, means the format is now crowded. The mascot or character (frog, cat, robot) is the differentiator, and it is also what fills the capsule and screenshots.
- Being framed as "productivity" or "company while you work" (Spirit City's pomodoro, Bongo Cat's keystrokes) gives a reason to keep the game open and links its visuals to the player's real activity.

### Gaps
- No official sales for Ropuka, Tiny Pasture, Desktop Defender or Chill Corner-type titles. Copies sold or revenue figures for Bongo Cat's cosmetics were not found.
- Whether transparent-window overlays outsell bounded-window ones was not found. Technical problems such as fullscreen conflicts and multi-monitor issues were not researched.

## UI/UX best practices and common complaints

### Takeaway
The recurring complaints are unreadable big numbers or poor notation, cluttered or hidden tabs, UI that becomes unreadable when the window is small, unintuitive interfaces and slow starts. Offline-progress screens are divisive. Best practice: gradual feature unlocks, clear tab states, a choice of notations, readable small-window layouts, and optional offline gains.

### Cited Findings
- Incrementals usually show huge values in scientific notation, shorthand suffixes or named schemes. — [Wikipedia, Incremental game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incremental_game)
- Alphabetical suffix notation is hard to read and gets longer as numbers grow. Antimatter Dimensions ships deliberately "painful" joke notations. — [NamuWiki](https://en.namu.wiki/w/%EC%A6%9D%EB%B6%84%20%EA%B2%8C%EC%9E%84)
- An itch.io commenter felt alphabetical naming weakened the sense of huge numbers and could confuse newcomers. — [itch.io post](https://itch.io/post/10474784)
- Player requests and complaints from itch.io threads:
  - Clearer active-tab styling, visible panel borders and tab hover effects.
  - Mobile browser scaling problems, and progress lost when switching browser tabs.
  - Starting in a prestige menu with nothing clickable.
  - A suggestion to unlock features gradually rather than all at once.
  - Sources: [itch.io post](https://itch.io/post/8310332); [itch.io post](https://itch.io/post/8312576); [galaxy.click](https://galaxy.click/play/729). The search summary did not map each point to a specific post.
- Offline progress: some players dislike offline gains (returning to big gains "feels disappointing") and want a toggle. One developer used offline gains so players wouldn't feel they had to leave the game running. Steam players expect offline income. A Revolution Idle offline popup misfired. — [itch.io](https://itch.io/post/10474784); [Idle Colony Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2843640/discussions/0/604142859631723610/?l=danish); [Revolution Idle Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2763740/discussions/0/4697908845438730655/?ctp=23)
- Small-window readability: a Spirit City player said at quarter-screen size "the UI and text become so small that a lot of it is not readable". Another asked about resizing the windowed game. — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2113850/discussions/0/4199112956964966820/); [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2113850/discussions/0/4326348922707908207/)
- Unnamed Space Idle reviewers cite an unintuitive interface and a steep learning curve. — [Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/2471100/unnamed_space_idle/stats/details)
- Antimatter Dimensions on Steam added zoom controls and a hotkey panel, which addresses dense-UI complaints. — [Steam community](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1399720/discussions/0/3732952742344527398)
- Rusty's Retirement was criticized for having no tutorial. — [Comfy Cozy Gaming](https://www.comfycozygaming.com/2024/05/03/rustys-retirement-review/)

### Inferences
- Notation is a branding choice as well as an accessibility setting. Default to readable suffixes or engineering notation, and offer scientific, letter and joke notations as options. A designed number font and pop style can be part of the game's identity.
- For desktop or overlay layouts, design UI scale for the smallest supported window first, and add UI zoom, as Antimatter Dimensions did.

- No sources on mobile-versus-desktop layout trade-offs or on the design of offline "welcome back" summary screens.

## Color palette and mood trends

### Takeaway
Two clusters dominate. One is warm, cozy pastel pixel art (desktop companions, lofi rooms). The other is dark, minimal, high-contrast geometric or neon (Nodebuster, Revolution Idle). The generic default to avoid in 2026 is a blue-toned sci-fi UI, which PC Gamer flagged as making new idlers "uncannily similar". I found little direct sourcing on CRT/terminal or eldritch palettes.

### Cited Findings
- Many idlers released in Sept 2026 share blue color schemes and look "uncannily similar". Handmade pixel art was singled out as distinctive. — [PC Gamer, 7 Sept 2026](https://www.pcgamer.com/gaming-industry/steam-week-in-review-54-idle-games-released-on-steam-last-week-and-many-look-eerily-similar/)
- Cozy, non-combat titles lead Steam idle revenue for 2024–2026. — [AppMagic](https://appmagic.rocks/research/idle-steam-games-2026)
- Spirit City is praised for warm lighting, VFX and cute companions. — [Steam store](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2113850/Spirit_City_Lofi_Sessions/); [LadiesGamers](https://ladiesgamers.com/spirit-city-lofi-sessions-review/)
- Gnorp's palette: one summary says mostly monochromatic, reviews say mainly muted with occasional vibrant accents. The sources conflict on emphasis. — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/interview-the-gnorp-apologue); [ComicBuzz](https://comicbuzz.com/the-gnorp-apologue-review/)
- Revolution Idle uses clean, minimalist colored circles. Nodebuster is described as having a "neon-lit, cybernetic" look, but that comes from a low-quality, likely machine-generated page. — [Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Free-to-play-this-free-idle-game-is-purely-addictive-and-climbs-to-1-on-the-Steam-charts.1009139.0.html); [gamegeeker](https://gamegeeker.com/games/nodebuster-3107330)
- A Dark Room: "a text-based game that gradually introduces you to the workings of a mysterious and brutal world". It shows a dark, sparse mood carried by text. — [Yahoo review](https://www.yahoo.com/news/dark-room-review-164648125.html)

### Inferences
- Openings for a distinctive identity: avoid blue sci-fi dashboards. Under-used combinations include warm and eldritch, CRT or terminal with juicy feedback, and hand-drawn or ink with incremental spectacle. Mixing a text or terminal aesthetic with juice could keep the minimalism while still producing a readable capsule.
- A muted base with saturated accents for gains (Gnorp-style) keeps busy screens readable and makes progress pop.

### Gaps
- No sourced data on dark-mode prevalence, CRT/terminal-style commercial results, or eldritch-themed idlers. These trends are asserted in the brief but were not confirmed by the sources I found.
